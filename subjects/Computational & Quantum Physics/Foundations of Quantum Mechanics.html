Section 01

Black-body radiation 🔥

Black body kya hai?

Ek ideal object jo incident radiation ko completely absorb karta hai aur apne temperature ke according radiation emit karta hai. Socho kisi object ko heat kar rahe ho: hot object → red → orange → yellow → white 🌡️

Classical problem 😵

Classical theory ke according high frequency par emitted energy continuously badhti jaani chahiye, yani I→∞. Experiments se match nahi hua — is failure ko bola gaya Ultraviolet Catastrophe.

Planck ka solution 💡

Max Planck ne kaha: energy continuously exchange nahi hoti — chhote packets mein hoti hai jinhe quanta kehte hain. General form E=nhf, aur ek quantum ke liye:

E=hf

where E = energy, h = Planck's constant (6.626×10−34 J·s), f = frequency.

🔑 Big idea: Energy is quantized — energy koi bhi arbitrary value nahi le sakti, sirf fixed packets mein exchange hoti hai.

Section 02

Photoelectric effect 💡

Jab light kisi metal surface par girti hai toh electrons eject ho sakte hain — inhe photoelectrons kehte hain. Classical wave theory expect karti thi: intensity badhao → energy badhe; strong light → electrons niklenge. Experiments ne kuch aur hi bataya:

1️⃣ Threshold frequency f0​

Isse kam frequency par electrons eject nahi hote — chahe intensity kitni bhi high ho.

2️⃣ Instantaneous emission ⚡

Frequency sufficient hai toh emission almost turant hota hai.

3️⃣ Kmax​ depends on frequency

Frequency badhao → emitted electrons ki max kinetic energy badhti hai; intensity se nahi.

Einstein ka jawab 🧠

Light photons (energy packets) ki tarah behave karti hai; har photon ki energy E=hf.
hf=ϕ+Kmax​⇒Kmax​=hf−ϕ

ϕ = work function (electron ko bahar nikalne ki minimum energy). Threshold frequency par: hf0​=ϕ.

Section 03 + 04

Wave–particle duality 🌊 & de Broglie

Light as wave 🌊

Interference & diffraction — pure wave behaviour.

Light as particle ⚛️

Photoelectric effect — pure particle behaviour (photons).
Dono sach hain! Light has both wave and particle nature — this is wave–particle duality.

de Broglie hypothesis 🪁

Louis de Broglie ne ulta sawal kiya: agar light particles jaisi behave kar sakti hai, toh matter particles bhi wave jaise behave kar sakte hain? Har moving particle ke saath ek wavelength judi hai — de Broglie wavelength:

λ=ph​=mvh​

λ = wavelength, p=mv = momentum. Cricket ball jaise bade object ke liye λ itni chhoti hai ki wave nature dikhta hi nahi; electron ke liye λ atomic scale tak pahunchti hai — isi liye quantum effects electrons par dominate karte hain.

Section 05

Heisenberg uncertainty 🎯

Classical physics mein hum sochte hain ki particle ki exact position aur exact momentum dono simultaneously measure kar sakte hain. Quantum mechanics bolta hai: donon ko unlimited precision ke saath kabhi nahi jaan sakte.

ΔxΔp≥2ℏ​=4πh​

yahan ℏ=2πh​ (reduced Planck constant). Agar position accurately naapi (Δx↓) toh momentum ki uncertainty badh jaati hai (Δp↑), aur vice-versa.

⚠️ Ye instruments ki kharabi nahi hai — ye quantum systems ki fundamental property hai. Nature itself "blurry" hai microscopic scale par.

Section 06

Wave function ψ & probability 🎲

Classical physics mein particle ki exact position batate hain. Quantum mechanics mein particle ko describe karta hai wave function: ψ. Lekin dhyan do — ψ khud probability nahi hai.

Born interpretation 📊

∣ψ∣2 = probability density — particle ko kisi position par paane ki likelihood.

Normalization ✅

Particle kahin na kahin definitely milega, isliye total probability = 1 hona chahiye.
∫∣ψ∣2dτ=1

Example (particle in a box): ψ sirf certain "allowed" shapes le sakti hai → energy bhi quantized nikalti hai. ∣ψ∣2 ke humps batate hain kahin particle milne ka chance zyada hai.

🗝️ ψ = quantum state ki poori jaankari; ∣ψ∣2 = "particle yahan milne ka chance". Certainty se probability — yehi shift is unit ka dil hai.

Section 07 + 08

Schrödinger equation 🌊 & operators 🛠️

Ab sawal: wave function time ke saath evolve kaise karti hai? Erwin Schrödinger ne iska equation diya — time-dependent form:

iℏ∂t∂ψ​=[−2mℏ2​∇2+V]ψ

Agar potential V time ke saath change nahi karta, toh time-independent form use hota hai — jo allowed energies aur wave functions deta hai:

−2mℏ2​∇2ψ+Vψ=Eψ⟺H^ψ=Eψ

Operators & observables 🛠️

Measurable quantity = observable (position, momentum, energy…). QM mein har observable ek operator se represent hota hai:

Positionx^=xMomentump^​=−iℏdxd​

Eigenvalue equation 🎯

Jab operator A^ kisi wave function par act kare aur wahi function wapas mile (sirf ek number se multiplied): A^ψ=aψ. Yahan ψ = eigenfunction, a = eigenvalue = wo value jo measurement mein mil sakti hai. Energy ke liye: H^ψ=Eψ.

Recap • I

Recap — the whole story (I)

1. Failure of classical physics

Classical physics explains macroscopic phenomena but fails for black-body radiation, the photoelectric effect, atomic stability and atomic spectra. These failures motivated quantum mechanics.

2. Black-body radiation & Planck

A black body absorbs all incident radiation and emits according to its temperature. Classical theory predicted unbounded intensity at high frequencies — the ultraviolet catastrophe. Planck proposed energy is exchanged in discrete quanta: E=hf, introducing the quantization of energy.

3. Photoelectric effect & Einstein

Electrons are emitted from a metal when light of sufficient frequency strikes it. Einstein explained this with photons of energy E=hf: Kmax​=hf−ϕ, where ϕ is the work function. The minimum frequency f0​ (with ϕ=hf0​) is the threshold frequency.

4. Wave–particle duality

Interference and diffraction show light's wave nature; the photoelectric effect shows its particle nature. Hence light possesses a dual wave–particle character.

5. de Broglie hypothesis

Matter particles also exhibit wave nature, with wavelength λ=ph​=mvh​ — the de Broglie wavelength, significant only for microscopic masses.

6. Heisenberg uncertainty principle

Exact position and exact momentum cannot be determined simultaneously with unlimited precision: ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2. This is a fundamental property of quantum systems, not an experimental limitation.

Recap • II

Recap — the whole story (II)

7. Wave function & Born interpretation

A quantum state is described by the wave function ψ(x,t). According to Born, ∣ψ∣2 is the probability density of finding the particle at a given position, and a valid state is normalized: ∫∣ψ∣2dτ=1.

8. Schrödinger equations

The time-dependent equation, iℏ∂ψ/∂t=H^ψ, governs how a quantum state evolves in time. For a time-independent potential the time-independent form, H^ψ=Eψ, yields the allowed energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions.

9. Operators & observables

Every measurable quantity (observable) is represented by an operator: x^=x, p^​=−iℏd/dx, E^=iℏ∂/∂t, H^=−2mℏ2​∇2+V. Acting on an eigenfunction, A^ψ=aψ, the eigenvalue a is a possible measured value.
💬 The essential shift: classical physics asked "where exactly is the particle and how exactly is it moving?" — quantum mechanics asks "what is the quantum state, what outcomes are possible, and with what probability?" From certainty to probability: that is the heart of Unit 1.

One-line summary per idea ✨

  • Planck: energy comes in packets, E=hf.
  • Einstein: light itself is quantized — photons.
  • de Broglie: matter has a wavelength, λ=h/p.
  • Heisenberg: nature limits simultaneous precision.
  • Born: ∣ψ∣2 = probability density.
  • Schrödinger: the equation of motion for ψ.
  • Operators: observables as mathematical machines; eigenvalues = measurable values.
